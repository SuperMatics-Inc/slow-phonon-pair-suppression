# Suppression of electronic pair order for slow lattice phonons: companion proofs

## Theorems and conventions

**PROVED (analytical).** On square nearest-neighbor tori with two electron spin species, independent neutral harmonic oscillators, and no additional electron interaction, use energy coordinates `b=F X`, `U=F²/K`, `Ω=ℏ√(K/m)`, `τ=k_B T`, `β=1/τ`. Use the real hopping `−t+b` on a bond for bond SSH, and `b n_i` and bare hopping `−t` for Holstein. `U,t` are fixed; the parameter sent to zero is `Ω`. `U=0` is the free model. A constant shift of the Holstein density can be absorbed by translating the oscillators and shifting the chemical potential. `Ω=0` means the normalized *annealed* static-coordinate Gibbs integral, not a trace of a multiplication operator on an oscillator Hilbert space. All free energies may subtract the free phonon free energy.

1. At `Ω=0`, on normalized antisymmetric two-particle space indexed by `i<j`, `0≤Γ≤I`. The isothermal pair-phase helicity tends to zero for fixed limiting canonical density `0<n<2` or fixed real finite grand-canonical chemical potential. The empty and full sectors have zero helicity at every volume; the full sector has `Γ=I`. No assertion is made at zero temperature or for a dynamical Drude weight.
2. Write `E_H=t`, `E_S=t+U`, `L_H=2^26`, and `L_S=2^40`. In the exact quantum model and at the static endpoint, the thermodynamic equal-time two-point correlation of fixed finite-support electronic fields of opposite nonzero even charge decays exponentially whenever

   `τ^4 ≥ L_j U Ω² (E_j+τ)`, `τ>0`.                                             (1)

   The grand-canonical bound is uniform in volume and real chemical potential. The canonical bound holds in periodic thermodynamic limits at fixed interior density. Define `T_pair` as the supremum of temperatures with algebraic correlations of such a fixed charge-two field, including fixed-momentum oscillations and multiplicative logarithms (zero if none). Then `k_B T_pair≤τ_*`, the unique positive solution of equality in (1) for positive `U,Ω`. This includes conventional electronic BKT detected by these fields without assuming its jump. It does not bound finite-mass helicity or the entire pair density matrix, or address order detected only by phonon-dressed operators. At fixed `t,U>0` the bound is `O(Ω^(1/2))`.

All one-body and Grassmann objects are finite-volume objects until the ensemble comparison. The constants are deliberately inflated. Finite algebra is replayed by `replay.py` and `lean/`; these do not formalize the analytic arguments.

## 1. Static model: pair density operator and helicity

For fixed `b`, diagonalize the real spin-independent one-body operator `h`. The fermion density matrix, even in the canonical ensemble, is a probability mixture of occupation configurations in that eigenbasis. The two-particle density operator is diagonal there, with eigenvalues `P(i and j occupied)≤1`. Unitary conjugation on the two-particle space and then averaging in the *electronic-partition-weighted* displacement law preserve the operator inequality. In the GC ensemble the state at fixed `b` is quasi-free with occupations `f_β(h−μ)`, but Gaussianity is not necessary for this argument. Bounded pair density alone is not our proof of zero helicity.

Take total electron twist `φ=φ_A+φ_B` across two parallel vertical cuts separated by `⌊L/2⌋` lattice steps on the square torus. Moving flux between cuts is an electronic unitary gauge change for each `b`. The two cut insertions are on disjoint edges, so `∂_A∂_B H=0`; each first derivative is the second quantization of a pure imaginary antisymmetric one-body matrix `j_A` or `j_B` in the site basis. Every first partition derivative at zero twist vanishes by transposition, including with complex fugacity. Thus the annealed second log curvature is the annealed expectation of the conditional second log curvature, without a displacement-covariance term. The pair-phase gradient `ϑ` has `φ=Lϑ/2`, so in two dimensions `ρ_s=−(τ/4)∂_A∂_B log Z`.

In the untilted static annealed law, each coordinate conditional on all the others has density proportional to `exp(−β b²/(2U)) Z_el(b)`, with `|∂_b log Z_el|≤2β`: differentiate the trace and use the norm of `n_i` or `B_b`. The bound holds even at fixed particle number. Divide by `Z_el(0)`, use the lower bound `exp(−2β|b|)` in the normalizer and `exp(2β|b|)` in the numerator. This gives uniform Gaussian tails (e.g. `C_β,U exp(−β R²/(4U))` after adjustment of the constant), and polynomial moments of the maximum on the tail by integration of that bound and the union bound. On `max|b|≤R_L=c√log(L+2)`, the one-body hopping magnitude is at most `J=t+R_L`, and the spectrum obeys `|h|≤H_*=4t+5R_L`. Choose `c` so large that the tail contribution vanishes: a crude conditional mixed log-curvature bound is `2β² ||dΓ(j_A)|| ||dΓ(j_B)||`, polynomial in `L` and `max|b|`, by thermal operator Hölder and the absence of a mixed Hamiltonian term. This handles the unbounded SSH hoppings without any bounded-support assumption on the physical displacement law.

For a static GC reference with a complex number angle `θ` (fugacity `exp(βμ+iθ)`), let `Z_θ=det(1+exp(−β(h−μ)+iθ))` on the `D=2L²` spin-orbitals. On `|θ|≤π/2`, the shifted antiperiodic frequencies have absolute values at least `π/(2β)`. Conjugate the one-body resolvent by `exp(a d(x,·))`, where `a=1/(1+16βJ)` and `d` is graph distance. The change in operator norm is at most `4J(e^a−1)≤8Ja≤1/(2β)` (Schur bound on rows and columns). The resolvent entries are therefore bounded by `2 exp(−a d)/|ω+θ/β|`. Differentiate the Fermi matrix using its paired Matsubara partial fractions. Explicitly the scalar identity for real `w` is `f_β(w)=1/2 + β^(−1) lim_(M→∞) Σ_(k=−M−1)^M (iω_k−w)^(−1)`, `ω_k=(2k+1)π/β`; it extends analytically off its poles, and its matrix derivative is an absolutely convergent sum of products of two resolvents. The mixed log derivative is, up to sign, `Σ tr(R j_A R j_B)`; hence it is bounded by a polynomial in `L,J,β` times `exp(−2a(⌊L/2⌋−2))`. The trace expansion connects both cuts twice; there is no diamagnetic term. `|Z_θ|≤Z_{θ=0}`. This already proves the GC static claim.

For the canonical trace, choose the *auxiliary real fugacity* separately for each `b` and hold it fixed while taking flux derivatives. Write `a_N=[z^N]det(1+ze^(−βh))` and choose `z=a_(N−1)/a_N` for `1≤N<D`. The coefficient sequence is log concave: multiplication by `1+wz` preserves log concavity without internal gaps, by expanding `b_k²−b_(k−1)b_(k+1)` for `b_k=a_k+w a_(k−1)`; the mixed term is `w(a_k a_(k−1)−a_(k−2)a_(k+1))≥0`. Thus `N` is a mode of the real-fugacity number distribution and `z^N a_N ≥ Z_0/(D+1)`. Counting extensions of an `(N−1)`-element subset gives `a_N/a_(N−1)` between `[(D−N+1)/N] exp(±βH_*)`; consequently `|μ|≤H_*+O_{n,β}(1)` on the cutoff. With real fugacity, `p_i=f_β(ε_i−μ)` and `v=Σ_i p_i(1−p_i)≥c_{n,β}D exp(−2βH_*)` for fixed interior limiting density. The constant includes the factor `1/4` from the Fermi variance lower bound.

On `π/2≤|θ|≤π` use the trace itself rather than resolvents (which may be singular). In a real eigenbasis for `h` each `j` has zero diagonal, also for degeneracies. In the mixed Duhamel derivative the two insertions must undo one another's single-orbital swap, so for each pair of modes the spectator trace contributes `∏_{k≠i,j}(1+e^(−β(ε_k−μ)+iθ))`. The absolute values of the two active occupation weights at intermediate times are bounded, after summing both choices, by `(1+e^(−β(ε_i−μ)))(1+e^(−β(ε_j−μ)))`: the two possible intermediate Boltzmann weights are geometric interpolants of the endpoint weights, whose sum is at most the sum of their endpoints. The simplex integrals and number of choices cost only a polynomial in `L,J,β`. Each spectator factor divided by its positive real counterpart is at most `exp(−p_k(1−p_k))`, since `|1−p+p e^{iθ}|²=1−4p(1−p)sin²(θ/2)`. The normalized mixed derivative thus costs at most a polynomial times `exp(−v+1/2)`. Extract `z^N a_N` by the circle integral of `e^(−iNθ)Z_θ`; the lower bound `Z_0/(D+1)` absorbs the normalization, and both parts of the circle vanish faster than any inverse power on `R_L=c√log(L+2)`. Together with the tail bound this proves zero canonical static helicity. This calculation does not interchange the mass and thermodynamic limits.

## 2. Exact opening of the slow-phonon limit

### 2.1. Integrate the nonzero temporal modes, not the static fields

Harmonic trace/Dyson expansion gives an independent periodic Gaussian field per oscillator with energy-coordinate covariance

`(U/β) Σ_(k∈Z) Ω²/(ν_k²+Ω²) exp(iν_k(u−v))`, `ν_k=2πk/β`.

The finite-volume matrix-valued harmonic Feynman–Kac formula applies in each electronic sector: the electronic Hilbert space is finite dimensional, and the electronic norm of the perturbation is bounded by a constant times the sum of absolute coordinates. Equivalently, truncate the linear potentials and pass to the limit by form convergence, using the Gaussian exponential moment of their integrated absolute value to dominate the electronic path weights.

This follows by Fourier diagonalizing the periodic inverse of `(−∂_u²+Ω²)` and multiplying by `UΩ²`. Equivalently it is the harmonic time-ordered covariance, and the exact expansion follows by Gaussian Wick at each order under the free-phonon trace and electronic time ordering. The Gaussian exponential moments for integrated absolute field at finite volume justify re-summing: Jensen in time and the marginal Gaussian moment bound give integrability for every finite linear coefficient. The zero mode is a scalar `b` with variance `U/β`, independent of the other modes. For `Ω=0` retain only this zero mode. The nonzero-mode covariance `D_*` has

`sup_(u,v) |D_*(u,v)| ≤ (U/β) Ω² × 2 (β/(2π))² Σ_(k≥1)1/k² = U β Ω²/12`.       (2)

Work in the time-ordered fermionic Wick expansion with one even monomial for each hopping or density operator. After integrating the nonzero modes one obtains the additional interaction exponent `+(1/2)Σ_α ∫du dv D_*(u,v) B_α(u)B_α(v)`; `B_α=n_i` for Holstein. Here the exponent denotes the time-ordered series, not an equal-time operator identity. Interacting electron propagation through this exponent is treated by a convergent connected expansion, not by replacing its covariance with a static or instantaneous attraction. See §2.3 for the precise estimates. This split is exact at every positive temperature.

### 2.2. Bound SSH reference hoppings without discarding the Gaussian tail

Holstein needs no cutoff at all: arbitrary real static on-site energies do not affect the resolvent estimate. For SSH only, let `R=4096(t+U+τ)`. Draw independently a reference value `x` from the Gaussian conditioned on `|x|≤R`. At each oscillator replace `x` with probability `p=P(|b|>R)` by an independently drawn tail variate; otherwise keep it. The resulting zero mode has exactly its original law. Conditional on the bounded reference `x`, the substitution multiplies the Wick integrand by `1+W_x`, where

`W_x = ∫_(|b|>R) dγ(b) [ exp(−(b−x) ∫_0^β B(u)du)−1 ]`.

This formula already uses the unconditional tail mass. Every hopping or density monomial is supported at the center or a nearest neighbor of the center (bond center taken as its tail site, with two orientations). Write a vertex with `2m` fermion legs and define its absolute weighted coefficient norm as the sum/integral of absolute coefficients times `exp(4m)`, per anchor site, summed over oscillator orientations. The norm is submultiplicative for formal products of time-ordered Wick integrands, with concatenated lists of legs; signs are immaterial. `B` has at most four unit-coefficient monomials. Hence

`||W_x|| ≤ ∫_(|b|>R) dγ(b) exp(4e^4 β |b−x|) ≤ 2 exp(−β R²/(8U))`.

Indeed `4e^4<256`, `|b−x|≤2|b|`, `R≥2048 U`, and `512|b|≤b²/(4U)` in the tail; split the remaining Gaussian exponent into two equal parts and integrate one. The formal exponent `log(1+W_x)=Σ_(r≥1) (−1)^(r+1) W_x^r/r` is absolutely convergent in this norm, and combines with the other exponent because even source monomials commute as labelled time-ordered products. Each tail exponent has norm at most `2||W_x||` since `||W_x||≤1/2`. The two bond orientations give a total tail norm at most `8 exp(−βR²/(8U))`. A vertex from (2) has `m=2`; its norm per oscillator is at most `(2/3)e^8 Uβ³Ω² ≤4096 Uβ³Ω²` (`16` from two bond operators, `1/2` from Gaussian integration, `1/12` from (2)). This bound uses no smallness of the *static* Gaussian field, and all series retain the annealed electronic partition weight at the last integration.

The majorants are uniform at fixed labels, leg offsets and times. Use a full time cube with factor `1/m!` for valence `2m` in `W_x`; the envelope for each specified coefficient on that cube is `∫_(|b|>R)(2|b|)^m/m! dγ(b)` for `m≥1`. Compose the nonnegative envelopes with `Σ_(r≥1) W^r/r` for the logarithm. Summing the four monomial choices per occurrence, integrating the times, and weighting by `e^(4m)` recovers the same tail budget. The quartic kernel is bounded pointwise before integrating or summing.

### 2.3. Uniform Wick connected expansion (details)

Let `J` bound the reference hopping and `h` include an arbitrary real diagonal and chemical potential. Set `a=(1+16βJ)^(-1)`. For a given site center let `W=e^(a d)` with `d` graph distance to that site. For `P=WhW^(-1)−h`, row and column sums give `||P||≤4J(e^a−1)≤8Ja`, so `β||P||≤1/2`; the same holds for `W^(-1)`. At odd frequencies `ω_k=(2k+1)π/β`, `||R||≤1/|ω_k|`, `||WRW^(-1)||≤2/|ω_k|` and the resolvent identity gives `||WRW^(-1)−R||≤2||P||/ω_k²`. The free ordered covariance `C_h(v)` has norm at most one by scalar spectral calculus. Its signed jump is scalar, so it cancels in the conjugated difference. The difference's antiperiodic resolvent series converges absolutely and agrees with both ordered one-sided endpoint limits. Since `β^(-1)Σ_k ω_k^(-2)=β/4`, the weighted covariance has norm at most `1+β||P||/2≤5/4`. Use `W` for a centered column and `W^(-1)` for a centered row. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds a weighted row/column sum with weight `exp(a d/2)` by `(5/4)(1+2/a)`, since `Σ_z exp(−a d(y,z))≤(1+2/a)^2` on the torus. Two endpoint shifts of at most one each cost `e^a`. Thus a fixed child-leg attachment summed over its anchor, at fixed template and times, costs at most `e^a(5/4)(1+2/a)<30(1+4βJ)≤Q=64(1+4βJ)`.

Use the following connected-expansion argument, stated to specify the determinant and combinatorial assumptions explicitly. For a Wick moment of labelled even vertices, multiply inter-vertex contractions by parameters `s_ij` and leave same-vertex contractions unchanged. For a forest with edges carrying `u_e∈[0,1]`, use `s_ij` equal to the smallest edge on the connecting path and zero between components, `s_ii=1`. The forest identity is the sum of integrals of the corresponding product of edge derivatives; at all entries one it recovers the Wick moment. To verify the identity for polynomials it suffices to take a monomial `∏ s_ij^{k_ij}` and form the multigraph with `k_ij` labelled copies of each edge. Give the copies independent uniform weights. In each connected component take its maximum spanning tree (unique almost surely). A non-tree copy must have weight below the least edge on its tree path, which is equivalent to that spanning tree being the greedy maximum tree. Integrating that event for each tree and choice of labelled copies yields exactly the forest integrals and proves the identity. The forest partition then factors by components: vertices have even degree in fermion fields, and no signs from moving whole even vertices between components. The cumulant (coefficient of the log of the Wick generating function) is precisely the sum over trees.

Each derivative of the Wick determinant chooses one unused field at each end of the forest edge. The total choices at a vertex with `2m` legs are at most `(2m)^degree`. The uncontracted minor has absolute value at most one, uniformly in degree, times, chemicals and reference coordinates. Indeed the matrix `s` above is positive semidefinite, the integral over `v∈[0,1]` of the partition equivalence matrices of edges with `u_e≥v`. Realize its diagonal-one Gram representation by unit vectors. In an auxiliary one-body space tensor the reference Hamiltonian with identity and replace each field test function by its tensor product with its vertex unit vector. The Wick minor is now a thermal time-ordered moment of creation/annihilation operators of norm at most one. Cyclic trace Hölder with exponents `β/Δu` between consecutive times gives a bound of one after dividing by the auxiliary thermal partition function (zero intervals use the operator norm). This proves the required determinant bound without an absolute determinant expansion or a time-frequency ultraviolet cutoff.

For clarity, a rooted majorant controls both convergence and decay. If `w_m` bounds the aggregate absolute coefficient mass of vertices with `2m` legs per site, and `B` bounds the sum of rooted attached subtrees per specified parent leg, attachment from a child site incurs `Q`; selecting the child leg costs at most `2m`; the `k` unordered children cost at most `(2mB)^k/k!`. The factorial follows by partitioning the labelled descendants among children in the original exponential generating series. Thus iteration in depth starts at zero and is dominated by `B ↦ Q Σ_m (2m) w_m exp(2m B)`. If `Q Σ_m w_m e^(4m)≤1/4`, the unit interval is invariant, since `2m≤e^m` for `m≥1`. A specified root of valence `2m` costs at most `exp(2m)` times its coefficient mass. The unrooted pressure log per site is bounded by the corresponding rooted sum (choose any one label as root and use `1/k!≤1/(k−1)!`); the same argument bounds the absolute Taylor series in extra sufficiently small sources, with a uniform radius in volume. These statements follow first for polynomials in finitely many vertices and then by the absolute bounds; the finite-volume unconnected series converges by the Wick bound `≤1` and exponentiation of the finite coefficient mass. The connected series exponentiates to that series by the forest factorization, so it is the actual analytic logarithm of the partition relative to the positive free reference. Time integrals and formal logarithms in §2.2 are justified by the same coefficient norms.

In applying the attachment estimate, first bound descendants uniformly in the parent leg and time, then sum the child anchor with its offsets and times fixed, and only then integrate the coefficient envelope from §2.2. A bound only on a fully integrated, displacement-dependent coefficient would not on its own justify this order.

For weighted charged insertions use two attachment bounds, `B_I` for an interaction parent leg and `B_S` for a source parent leg. For fixed source support radius `R_s`, a source-parent to ordinary-child edge costs at most `C_R Q` for some `C_R≥1`, by the same envelope with endpoint shifts of at most `R_s+1`. On `0≤B_I≤1`, `0≤B_S≤C_R`, the ordinary-child parts cost at most `1/4` and `C_R/4`, respectively. The source-child terms are bounded by finite edge sums times their source amplitudes and `(2m) exp(2m B_S)`. Since the source valences are fixed and finite, shrinking the source disk leaves the rectangle invariant without changing the interaction-only condition. Root one charged insertion and give translations of the opposite-charge source a common small majorant. The coefficient linear in the latter is absolutely summable with weight `e^(a d/2)` on separation by the triangle inequality along the unique tree path. Each individual charged expectation vanishes at zero sources by number conservation; the mixed log coefficient is their correlation. Equal-time order is unambiguous for disjoint supports.

For the pressure take a uniform Hermitian bilocal source `v Σ_x O_{x,r}` at fixed separation `r`, and separately its imaginary combination. Use unweighted trees and measure attachment from the actual parent leg rather than its center. The ordinary-child sum for any parent is bounded by `Q`, irrespective of `r`; the source-child sum at fixed `r` and valence is finite. The same recursion with a shrunk disk gives a uniform per-volume majorant on the absolute log series. Terms of degree at least two in `v` are bounded by `c_r |v|²` on a smaller disk; the linear coefficient has the charged decay just proved and a factor `β`. The constants and disk may depend on fixed `r` in the quadratic estimate, not in the first-order decay prefactor.

For real `v` these conditional partition functions are positive near zero: the connected series has no zeros there; reality follows by reversing imaginary time and taking the adjoint of the ordered electronic trace, since the path law conditional on `x` is invariant under time reflection and the number-conserving Hamiltonian source is Hermitian. At zero, positivity follows either by continuity from the reference as the residuals are scaled in the convergent disk, or from the real identical-spin determinant square for the no-source integrand before integrating the oscillators. Thus averaging over base `x` with its *actual zero-source partition weight* preserves the two-sided pressure inequality

`|V^(−1) (log Z_GC(v)−log Z_GC(0))| ≤ |v| c e^(−a d(0,r)/2) + c_r |v|²`.      (3)

Here `v` is a Hamiltonian coefficient; its `β` factor may be included in `c,c_r`. The direct GC correlation bound follows by the same averaging. This proof allows long-range neutral ordering or coexistence; no electron/phonon ensemble is claimed unique.

### 2.4. Numbers and fixed density

For Holstein `J=t`, `Q≤2^8(1+βt)`, and the quantum product is at most `2^20 Uβ³Ω²(1+βt)`. For SSH let `x_*=β(t+U+τ)≥1`; `J≤4097(t+U+τ)`, `1+4βJ≤2^15 x_*`, so `Q≤2^21 x_*`. Count two orientations to get `2^34 Uβ³Ω² x_*`, with tail product at most `2^24 x_* exp(−2^21 x_*)<1/64`. The tail inequality uses `log x_*≤x_*`, `e>2` and `βR²/(8U)≥2^21 x_*`. In both models (1) gives quantum product at most `1/64` because `β³(1+βE)=(E+τ)/τ^4`. The margin below `1/4` permits the needed complex source disk. For `A=L_j UΩ²>0`, `τ^4/(E+τ)` has derivative `τ³(4E+3τ)/(E+τ)^2>0`. It ranges from zero to infinity. With `s=max((2AE)^(1/4),(2A)^(1/3))`, each of `AE,As` is at most `s^4/2`, and hence `(AE)^(1/4)≤τ_*≤s`. The equation gives `τ_*=(L_jUE)^(1/4)√Ω+O(Ω)` for fixed `t,U>0`. For `Ω=0`, (1) holds for all `τ>0`; `U=0` is the free case.

Here is the canonical transfer used in the theorem. For `v=0`, the canonical free pressure equals the Legendre transform of the GC pressure at any interior limiting density; at a coexistence density choose any supporting `μ`, not a unique tuning solution. A short thermodynamic justification in these models is as follows. Removing or adding a boundary hopping costs in `log Z` at most `2βt` per bond. A cut SSH electron-phonon link costs at most `4β U` per bond when interpolating the coupling `F→sF`: the harmonic Gaussian/Duhamel identity gives `|⟨F X_b B_b⟩|≤s U ||B_b||²≤4sU` (the full covariance has integral `U`, and the same-operator Duhamel correlator is bounded by `||B_b||²` by trace Hölder). In the static model this is simply Gaussian integration by parts and the integral of the static covariance. These bounds are uniform in volume, temperature fixed, and particle sector. Dividing out free oscillators makes tiling additive up to these surface bounds. With cuts made, the fixed-number partition is the sum over all block-number allocations of products; for `k×k` blocks its log differs from the maximum by at most the number of blocks times `log(2k²+1)`. The maximizing sum per volume converges, as the number of such blocks grows, to the concave envelope of the block log partitions per site, evaluated at the prescribed density: approximate any convex combination by rational fractions of blocks; the leftover number adjustment is `O(k²)` for fixed block size and costs only a vanishing fraction of blocks. Open boundary and periodic perimeters, including a leftover strip, contribute `O(1/k)` per volume (sector free energies are bounded by a volume constant from the same link estimates and the on-site completed square). Letting `k→∞` proves existence, concavity, and continuity in the interior by matching limsup and liminf; the log of the GC sum differs from its maximum over sectors by at most `log(2V+1)`, giving the stated duality. In this argument density is total electron number per site and the Legendre slope has the factor `β` for `log Z`.

Let the supporting slope of the limiting canonical normalized log partition as a function of `n` be `−βμ`. Define `δ_L=V^(−1)(log Z_GC(0;μ)−log Z_can,N(0)−βμN)≥0`, which vanishes by duality. For `v>0` the two finite-volume secants bound the canonical derivative in absolute value by `c exp(−a d(0,r)/2)+c_r v+δ_L/v`, using `Z_can,N(±v)e^(βμN)≤Z_GC(±v;μ)` and (3). Take volume to infinity at fixed `v,r`, then send `v→0` at fixed `r`, and only then let the separation grow. Both real and imaginary expectations have the exponential bound. Periodic translation invariance identifies the derivative per volume with the expectation at the designated separation. This works at neutral coexistence. At `n=0` or 2 the limiting electronic state is locally trivial by the density and particle/hole inequalities.

## 3. Combining with the bare-parameter stiffness theorem

**PROVED.** Write `x=Ω/t`, `u=U/t`, `d=min(n,2−n)`, `e=E_j/t`, `λ_FS=a_j(n)u` and `Y=C_inf x√λ_FS`. Take `C_inf=√supσ(G_2)/(2π)` with the explicit soft-kernel matrix in §5 of the manuscript (Kiessling, Altshuler and Yuzbashyan, DOI `10.1007/s10955-025-03446-5`). Substitution into (1) gives `Y^2/(e+Y)≥L_j/(C_inf²a_j(n))` as a sufficient comparison. Separately the equilibrium diamagnetic bounds derived in the manuscript give `ρ_s≤td/2` (Holstein) or `ρ_s≤(t/2+U)d` (SSH); assuming the conventional BKT jump it suffices that `Y≥πd/4` or `Y≥πd(1+2u)/4`, respectively. No bound from scalar ME at this singular square-lattice DOS is assumed. The per-spin Fermi-surface benchmark is not the global DOS-height theorem.
